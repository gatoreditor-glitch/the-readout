A SeekingAlpha piece this morning argues the market is showing "1999-like valuations" alongside "2007-like credit issues." ([Source](https://finnhub.io/api/news?id=edfad3c74cf986abb114d7043ae6db480a40853c3d916b6b0104e7f70941a6ee))

Catchy. Both years ended badly, so the pairing is designed to get your attention. But analogies are cheap and gauges are not. Let's run each historical template through this morning's System Signals dashboard and see which one actually fits.

## The 1999 template: euphoria

1999 wasn't just expensive -- it was euphoric. Vertical momentum, retail piling in, everyone all-in. Valuation was the symptom; mania was the disease.

Check today's momentum gauge: the S&P 500 is up 2.9% over the last three months. That is not euphoria. That is a market treading water. On the valuation half, there may be something to the comparison -- this morning's wire also notes the S&P 500's earnings yield jumping over 5%, which works out to roughly a 20x multiple on trailing earnings. Not cheap. But expensive is not the same as euphoric, and the sentiment half of the 1999 template is simply missing.

## The 2007 template: seizing credit

2007's tell came from the credit market first: an inverted yield curve, then funding markets seizing up, then everything else. The equity damage was the last chapter, not the first.

Check today's curve gauge: the 10-year minus 2-year spread is +0.33 percentage points. Normally sloped. No inversion, no recession warning from the curve. Realized volatility sits at 10.2% -- calm, not stressed. The 2007 fingerprints aren't on this market either.

## What neither analogy names

Here's the original read: both analogies are pointing at the wrong risk. The dashboard has exactly one bearish gauge -- inflation at 3.7%, hot enough to keep the Fed on hold indefinitely.

High inflation doesn't cause the crash. It removes the safety net. In both 1999 and 2001, and again in 2008, rate cuts cushioned the fall once trouble started. With CPI at 3.7% and drifting the wrong way, the Fed has very little room to play that role this time. That's the condition under which valuation problems *become* 1999 problems and credit problems *become* 2007 problems -- not the trigger, but the reason there's no fire extinguisher handy.

## To be fair

Analogies are warnings, not forecasts, and dismissing them outright would be lazy. Valuations are rich by any historical standard, and credit conditions can deteriorate faster than any dashboard refreshes -- 2007's curve inverted months before the real damage began. The gauges describe today accurately. They don't promise anything about six months from now. Respect the warning; verify it against the data as it comes in.

## Here's your plan

1. **Watch the two gauges that would validate each half.** Credit spreads widening would confirm the 2007 side of the story. Earnings revisions rolling over would confirm the 1999 side. Until one of those moves, they're stories, not signals.
2. **Keep the inflation gauge front and center.** It's the one flashing red, and it's the reason the Fed can't quickly bail out either scenario.
3. **Don't trade the analogy.** Trade what the gauges say: mixed trend, calm volatility, steady labor at 4.2% unemployment, hot inflation. That's a market to be selective in -- not one to flee, and not one to chase.

History rhymes, but it doesn't repeat on schedule. The gauges are how you tell the difference between a rhyme and a rerun.
