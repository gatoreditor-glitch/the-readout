"As January goes, so goes the year." The January barometer is one of Wall Street's oldest sayings -- and since 2010, it's been wrong in the way that matters most. Six Januarys have finished down in that span. In five of the six, the market *still* finished the rest of the year higher. Median gain from February through December: +15.5%.

## The numbers

- **Setup:** the S&P 500 finishes January with a loss for the month.
- **What followed:** a median gain of **+15.5%** over the rest of the calendar year.
- **Hit rate:** positive **83%** of the time -- 5 out of 6.
- **Sample:** 6 episodes, 2010 through 2026.

## What it means

A down January feels like a verdict on the year. The base rate says it's barely a hint. Markets that stumble in January have, more often than not, recovered and then some -- because eleven months is a long time, and because the same dip-buying dynamics that power post-correction rebounds apply to early-year weakness too. The old barometer may have worked in some earlier era; in the post-2010 sample, a down January has been a buying opportunity far more often than a warning.

## The fine print

Six episodes is a thin sample, and the barometer's reputation wasn't built on nothing -- across much longer histories, the January effect has shown *some* predictive power, roughly speaking. What the post-2010 data shows is that the relationship has broken down in the modern era of activist central banks and dip-buying algorithms. Also note the selection effect: a down January often *causes* the policy response (or the bargain-hunting) that drives the subsequent rally. Don't treat this as a law -- treat it as evidence that the burden of proof is on the bears, not the bulls, after a weak January.

## How to use it

When January ends red and the annual forecasts get hastily revised downward, check this base rate before revising your own plans. The historical playbook says: don't extrapolate one month into twelve. If you were constructive on the year in December, a down January is historically a better entry point than an exit signal. Save your caution for deteriorating trend and momentum gauges -- the calendar month is one of the weakest predictors in the database.
