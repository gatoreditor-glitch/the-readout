A +3% day feels like vindication -- proof the market is back, the bottom is in, the rally is real. Since 2010 there have been nine such days (as distinct episodes). Three months later the market was higher 67% of the time, with a median gain of +7.5%. Encouraging -- until you ask *when* those days happened.

## The numbers

- **Setup:** the S&P 500 rises 3% or more in a single session (clustered episodes counted once).
- **What followed:** a median gain of **+7.5%** over the next 63 trading days (about three months).
- **Hit rate:** positive **67%** of the time -- 6 out of 9.
- **Sample:** 9 episodes, 2010 through 2026.

## What it means

Explosive up days are momentum ignitions: they mark either the start of a powerful advance or a violent short-covering rally. On average, the follow-through has been positive -- a +3% day more often launches something than ends it.

## The fine print

This is the most regime-dependent setup in the database, and the headline numbers hide it. The biggest single-day rallies in market history cluster *inside bear markets* -- 2008 and 2020 both produced strings of +3% days on the way to lower lows. A +3% day with the market above its 200-day average is a momentum ignition worth respecting. A +3% day with the market 20% below its high is often a bear-market rally -- the market's way of punishing shorts before resuming the decline. Nine episodes is also a small sample, and 67% is the market's background hit rate over three months anyway. The number alone doesn't discriminate; the regime does.

## How to use it

Never read this setup without checking the trend regime gauge first. Above the 200-day: treat the +3% day as confirmation -- the uptrend has energy, and pullbacks are for buying. Below the 200-day: treat it as suspect -- enjoy the bounce, but don't mistake a short squeeze for a new bull market. The base rate's +7.5% median is real, but it was earned mostly by the episodes that fired in healthy markets. Context is the entire trade.
