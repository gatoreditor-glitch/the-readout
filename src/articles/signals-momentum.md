In 1993, two finance professors documented something that shouldn't exist in an efficient market: stocks that had risen over the prior 3 to 12 months kept rising, and losers kept losing. Thirty years and hundreds of follow-up studies later, momentum remains one of the most replicated findings in all of finance.

The dashboard's momentum gauge applies that same idea to the whole market: the S&P 500's total return over the last 63 trading days -- one calendar quarter.

## What it measures

Sixty-three trading days of S&P 500 price change, expressed as a percentage. On this dashboard:

- **Bullish:** above +5% for the quarter. The market has real forward motion.
- **Bearish:** below −5%. Sustained selling pressure, not a one-day event.
- **Neutral:** between −5% and +5%. Drifting, coiling, waiting.

## Why it works

Momentum exists because information travels slowly and humans herd. Good news takes weeks to get fully priced in as analysts revise, funds accumulate, and skeptics convert. The same machinery runs in reverse on the way down. A quarter is long enough to separate a real move from a noisy week, and short enough to still describe the market you're actually trading.

## To be fair

Momentum is the factor most prone to violent reversal. In early 2009, momentum strategies suffered some of their worst losses on record as the most beaten-down stocks snapped back hardest -- the "momentum crash." Crowded momentum also ends in tears: when everyone is leaning the same way, there's nobody left to push. And a +5% quarter can mark the middle of a run or the exhaustion of one; the gauge can't tell you which. Treat strong momentum as confirmation of trend, not as a green light to chase.

## How to use it

Read momentum alongside trend regime. When both agree -- price above its averages AND a strong quarter -- the market's advance has broad, durable support. When they disagree, dig deeper: strong momentum below the 200-day is often a bear-market rally; weak momentum above the 200-day is a market running out of steam. And when momentum prints an extreme quarter, up or down, start watching for the turn instead of betting on more of the same.
