Consumer prices rose 3.7% year-over-year in September, the latest data show — up from 2.9% twelve months earlier and nearly double the Federal Reserve's 2% target.

![CPI inflation, year-over-year](/assets/charts/cpi-yoy.png)

## What the chart shows

The five-year view tells the story in one line: inflation collapsed from its 9.0% peak, bottomed near 2.3%, and has since been grinding higher for a year — 2.9% to 3.3% to 3.5% to 3.7%. Not a spike. A drift. And drifts are harder for policymakers to dismiss than one-off jumps.

## Why it matters

A 3.7% inflation rate keeps the Fed in hawkish territory. Every tenth of a point away from target is another reason to hold rates higher for longer — which is why this gauge reads bearish on our System Signals dashboard. For markets, the path of inflation matters more than the level: disinflation rallies, re-acceleration sells off.

## What to watch next

The next CPI release and the shelter component within it — housing costs are the stickiest part of the basket. If the drift continues into year-end, expect rate-cut expectations to keep getting pushed out.
