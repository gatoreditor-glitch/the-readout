Read the six gauges on The Readout's dashboard this morning and you will see exactly what you saw on Saturday and exactly what you saw on Sunday. SPY at $735.29, below its 50-day average and above its 200-day. Three-month momentum at -4.9%. Realized volatility at 19.7%. The yield curve at +0.38 percentage points. Inflation at 3.7%. Unemployment holding around 4.1-4.2%.

Three straight mornings, six identical readings. The dashboard is not broken. It is just measuring the wrong risk today.

## What the gauges do say

The scoreboard reads two bullish, one bearish, three neutral. The yield curve and the labor market are the bullish pair: a normally sloped curve means the bond market is not flashing a recession warning, and jobs are holding around 4.1-4.2%. The bearish gauge is inflation at 3.7%, hot enough to keep the Fed from riding to the rescue. Everything else is neutral, which is the honest label for a market that fell hard one day, bounced the next, and has now gone nowhere for a quarter.

Volatility at 19.7% converts to an average daily move of about 1.24% -- twice the late-October weather, and still a choppy market, not a broken one.

Here is the number that matters. SPY's 52-week high is $781.62, so Friday's close of $735.29 puts the market 5.9% below it. The base-rate database says a 10% drawdown from the high has been the best buying setup since 2010: a median gain of 15.6% over the next 12 months, positive 93% of the time across 15 episodes. At 5.9% down, that setup has not fired -- the market would need to fall roughly another $32, to about $703, first.

## What the gauges cannot see

None of the six gauges measures credit. None of them measures concentration. None of them measures what happens when the single most important company in the market has a bad morning.

Today's wire is a catalog of exactly those risks. Banks and investors are growing wary of AI debt after the market selloff. CoreWeave is confronting a "doom cycle" narrative as neocloud credit risk overshadows its growth. And Nvidia -- the company whose valuation ran from $420 billion in late 2022 to $4.5 trillion as of November 6 -- faces a board probe into its chief executive, with shares down about 6% in pre-market trading.

A dashboard built on index prices, volatility, inflation, jobs, and the curve will not flinch at any of that until the damage reaches the index. That is by design: a 20-day volatility window, a 63-day momentum window, monthly inflation and jobs prints. They tell you the weather, not whether the wiring in the walls is fraying.

## To be fair: calm gauges have been right before

The counterpoint is that this split has resolved calmly more often than not. The curve is not inverted, jobs are not deteriorating, and 5.9% off the high is a normal pullback. The base rates say the setups that follow real fear have not triggered yet.

Today's wire may still be noise the index absorbs by Friday. The dashboard's refusal to move is, in its own way, a forecast: the broad market has not yet decided this matters.

## Here's your plan

Watch three things this week, and none of them is on the dashboard. First, the 200-day line under SPY -- the last line in the trend gauge, and the one that turns a neutral reading bearish if it breaks. Second, the credit story: if AI-debt wariness spreads from the neoclouds to the big hyperscalers' borrowing costs, that is a market event the gauges will only catch late. Third, whether Nvidia's pre-market slide holds at the open or reverses -- a 6% drop that sticks would be the index's problem, not just Nvidia's.

The dashboard will move when the tape moves. Until then, read it for what it is: a calm weather report on a morning when the risk is in the wiring, not the weather.
