Four days ago this dashboard flipped bullish. Five gauges green, one red, and the S&P 500 proxy sitting $1.66 above its 50-day average. That flip is gone.

This morning the dashboard reads three bullish, two neutral, one bearish. SPY sits at $755.72, back below its 50-day average but still above its 200-day. And the momentum gauge did not just fade -- it swung from +5.5% over three months to -0.3%. That is a 5.8-point round trip in four days, and it is the single most honest number on the board today.

## What changed, gauge by gauge

The trend gauge is the first reversal. Above the 50-day and above the 200-day is an uptrend; below the 50-day and above the 200-day is a market in between. In between is where this market lives now, and in-between markets punish both directions: buyers who treat the 200-day as a floor get tested, and sellers who treat the 50-day as a ceiling get squeezed.

Momentum confirms the trend gauge rather than contradicting it, which is the worse version of this story. A price dip with momentum still positive is a pullback. A price dip with three-month momentum at -0.3% is a market that has given back a quarter's progress. The two neutral gauges are telling the same story from different angles: no trend, no tailwind.

The three bullish gauges have not moved, and that matters. Realized volatility sits at 11.3% on a 20-day annualized basis, measured as a population standard deviation -- calm, by any reading. The yield curve, 10-year minus 2-year, sits at +0.37 percentage points, a normal upward slope with no recession warning in it. Unemployment is holding around 4.1-4.2% on the July, August, and September prints. None of these describe a market breaking. They describe a market waiting.

The one bearish gauge is the same one that has blocked this market all month. CPI year over year sits at 3.7%, hot enough to keep the Fed hawkish. That is the tension in one sentence: the backdrop gauges say conditions support risk, and the inflation gauge says the central bank still has a reason to say no.

## To be fair: a split board is not a sell signal

Three bullish, two neutral, one bearish is not a rout. Friday's snapshot, the latest on the site's market board, had the S&P 500 proxy down 1.77% and the Nasdaq 100 proxy down 3.47% -- an ugly day, but one that arrived with volatility still at 11.3%, not spiking. Markets that fall without volatility spiking are repricing, not panicking. And the 200-day line below is still intact, which is the difference between a correction and a trend change. Every gauge that flipped this week can flip back on one strong week. The October 28 flip proved how fast that happens; this weekend's unwind proves it again, in the other direction.

## Here's your plan

Watch three things, in order. First, the 200-day: as long as SPY holds above it, this is a trading range with a hot-inflation ceiling, not a new downtrend. Second, the momentum sign: a move back above zero on the three-month return would mean buyers are winning quarters again, not just days. Third, the next CPI print: at 3.7%, inflation is the only gauge with the power to turn the other five. If it cools, the Fed gets room and the split board resolves bullish. If it holds hot, the ceiling stays.

Until one of those three breaks, the right posture is the dashboard's own: split. Neither all-in nor all-out, because the gauges themselves cannot agree -- and when your instruments disagree, the trade is to wait for them to agree, not to pick a side for them.
