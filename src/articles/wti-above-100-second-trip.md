U.S. crude is holding above $100 this morning. Front-month WTI futures traded at $101.04, up 1.6%, as of 6:51 a.m. ET, while Brent -- the international benchmark -- hit $114.49, a five-month high. The trigger: Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said Thursday that four vessels had been struck and caught fire in the Persian Gulf, with three more hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran's army chief announced the military is fully prepared for "asymmetric warfare."

([Source: CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/oil-price-brent-wti-iran-war-saudi-arabia.html))

For The Readout, the crude benchmark is WTI, so let's talk WTI. This is the sixth trip above $100 this year -- and the last sustained one taught an expensive lesson.

## The number that matters: +88.6%

WTI started the year at $57.21 (January 2). It touched $107.89 on October 20, per FRED's spot series. That's an 88.6% climb in under ten months, with the year's high at $114.58 back on April 7 -- during the year's earlier above-$100 runs (the longest stretched 10 trading days, May 11–22). This morning's futures print extends the current run to a seventh trading session (Oct 15–23, per daily spot data through Oct 20 plus this week's futures prints).

Now the history that matters. The last time WTI held above $100 for a sustained stretch was March through July 2022 -- the Ukraine-invasion shock. I ran the tape: from the start of that run on March 1, 2022, the S&P 500 slid as much as 14.9%, bottoming mid-June, and closed the window on July 29 still down 4.2%. Above-$100 oil didn't just dent stocks. It overlapped with one of the worst drawdowns of the decade.

That's the systems read: sustained triple-digit oil is a tax on everything downstream. It shows up in margins first -- remember yesterday's P&G and American Airlines reports, both blaming input costs -- then in CPI, then in what the Fed can and can't do.

## To be fair: this isn't 2022, and the market knows it

Two honest counterpoints. First, the 2022 drawdown wasn't oil alone -- it was the invasion, the fastest rate-hike cycle in decades, and a valuation reset all arriving at once. Oil was the accelerant, not the whole fire.

Second, this morning's market is not panicking. WTI is actually on track for a weekly decline of 1.1%, snapping two straight weekly gains -- the +1.6% bounce today is a rebound, not a breakout. And President Trump publicly accused Iran of fabricating the attacks to lift prices ahead of the midterms, writing that "more Oil is coming through the Hormuz Strait now than it did even prior to the beginning of the Conflict." If the physical flow is intact, the risk premium can unwind fast. Brent is on its third straight weekly gain, though, so someone is paying for insurance.

## Here's your plan

Don't trade the headline -- trade the duration. The 2022 lesson wasn't that oil spikes; it was that oil *stays*. The number to watch is the length of this above-$100 run: April's run lasted 12 days before breaking. If this one stretches past two weeks, start pricing the 2022 playbook -- margin pressure, hot CPI prints, a Fed with no room to cut. And quote WTI when you do it: $101.04 this morning is the number that decides the American consumer's next quarter, not Brent's $114.49.
