Nothing makes investors more nervous than a market at all-time highs. It *feels* like the top -- everything you own is up, the gains feel borrowed, and some ancient instinct whispers "sell." Since 2010, the S&P 500 has printed 36 distinct new 52-week highs. Eighty-one percent of the time, it was higher a year later. The median gain: +13.2%.

## The numbers

- **Setup:** the S&P 500 closes at a new 52-week high (clustered -- repeat highs within two months count once).
- **What followed:** a median gain of **+13.2%** over the next 12 months.
- **Hit rate:** positive **81%** of the time -- 29 out of 36.
- **Sample:** 36 episodes, 2010 through 2026. The largest sample in the database.

## What it means

New highs are confirmation, not exhaustion. A market making new highs is, by definition, in an uptrend -- and uptrends persist. The investors who sold every new high since 2010 spent a decade watching the market leave without them. "The trend is your friend" is a cliche because the data keeps voting for it: with 36 episodes, this is the most statistically sturdy base rate we have.

## The fine print

Eighty-one percent is not 100%. Seven of these episodes were followed by 12-month losses -- and new highs do cluster near major tops. The highs of late 2007 and early 2022 both preceded serious damage. The base rate can't distinguish a healthy breakout from a blowoff top in real time; that's a job for the trend and momentum gauges, not the calendar. Also note the asymmetry: the median *gain* after a new high (+13.2%) is slightly lower than after a 10% correction (+15.6%). You pay for certainty -- buying strength costs more than buying weakness, on average.

## How to use it

When the market hits a new high and your finger hovers over the sell button, check this base rate first. The odds say the high is a waypoint, not a destination. Use new highs as permission to stay invested and to keep riding winners rather than rebalancing away your best performers prematurely. The time to get cautious isn't the high itself -- it's when the trend and momentum gauges start deteriorating *after* the high.
