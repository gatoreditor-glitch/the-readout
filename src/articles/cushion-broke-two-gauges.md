Yesterday this dashboard warned that the market's cushion was thin. This morning it is gone.

The S&P 500 proxy SPY closed Friday at $755.72, down $13.64 -- about 1.8% -- from $769.36 the day before. That single drop did two things at once: it pushed SPY back below its 50-day average, flipping the trend gauge from bullish to neutral, and it dragged three-month momentum from +3.0% to -0.3%, flipping that gauge to neutral as well. Two gauges, one session.

## What actually broke, and what did not

The trend break is real but shallow. Yesterday SPY sat $1.73 above its 50-day line of $767.63 -- the toe-over-the-line position this site flagged as fragile. Today it sits below that line, so the short-term trend has turned down again. But the long-term trend has not broken: SPY at $755.72 still sits roughly 3.7% above its 200-day average near $728.53. The market fell out of the attic, not through the floor.

Momentum tells the same story with a longer memory. Three-month momentum is now -0.3%, meaning the S&P 500 has gone slightly backwards over the last 63 trading days. A 63-day average does not usually move 3.3 points in a day. This one did, which tells you how much of the last three months' gain was concentrated in the recent run -- and how fast a rout can rewrite the medium-term picture.

Here is the number that should surprise you: realized volatility is 11.3%. Yes, it rose from 9.8% yesterday. But 11.3% annualized is still calm by any honest standard. A market that just dropped nearly 2% in a day, with tech leading the fall on AI spending fears, is not panicking by this measure. It is repricing. Those are different things, and the distinction is worth money: panics overshoot and reverse; repricings grind until the new price is accepted.

The rest of the board did not move. The yield curve still slopes upward at +0.37 percentage points -- no recession warning from the bond market. Consumer prices still run 3.7% year over year, hot enough to keep the Federal Reserve hawkish. Unemployment is holding around 4.1-4.2%, steady.

## To be fair: one day is a data point, not a verdict

A single close below the 50-day average has cried wolf before, and recently. Markets cross their short-term trend lines, get pulled back, and cross them again. Yesterday's flip to bullish lasted exactly one session. Today's flip back to neutral could last one session too.

The counterpoint cuts the other way as well. The momentum gauge does not flip on noise -- a 63-day average moving 3.3 points in a day means the recent gains were thinner than they looked. If you only watched the trend gauge, you could dismiss today as a wobble. The momentum gauge says the wobble landed on something soft.

## Here's your plan

Watch two lines next week, and ignore most of the rest. First, the 50-day average overhead: a close back above it turns the trend gauge constructive again and marks this as a one-day scare. Second, the 200-day average near $728 underneath: that is the floor that decides whether this is a pullback or a trend change. The site's own base-rate history says first breaks below that line have usually marked fear worth buying rather than the start of something worse, with a +13.2% median gain over the following six months in 15 episodes since 2010 -- history, not a promise, but a reason not to panic-sell on a headline if the floor gives way.

And check volatility before you check your portfolio. If realized vol stays near 11% while prices fall, the market is orderly and your plan can be orderly too. If vol spikes toward 20%, the tape is changing character, and so should your urgency.
