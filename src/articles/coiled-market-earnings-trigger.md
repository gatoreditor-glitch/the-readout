Three-month return: +2.9%. Realized volatility: 10.2%.

Read those three numbers together and you get the profile of a market holding its breath. Not breaking down. Not breaking out. Just... compressed. And compressed markets have a habit of resolving suddenly, in whichever direction the next real catalyst pushes them.

## What the gauges are saying

Start with trend: price is below its 50-day average and above its 200-day. The short-term crowd is cautious, the long-term crowd is patient, and neither has enough evidence to win the argument.

Now add momentum: +2.9% over three months. Positive, but anemic. This market isn't running away from anything -- or toward anything.

And then volatility: 10.2% annualized over the last 20 days. That is genuinely calm. No panic, no euphoria, no forced selling.

Put it together and the dashboard is describing stored energy. Low volatility plus weak momentum plus a mixed trend is the market equivalent of a coiled spring. The only thing missing is the trigger.

## Why this week could be the trigger

Big Tech earnings land this week, and in a coiled market, earnings season is the classic catalyst. It forces repricing -- up or down -- because it replaces speculation with numbers.

Here's the part most investors get wrong about earnings week: the numbers themselves matter less than the market's reaction to them. Strong results met with selling is distribution -- smart money using good news to exit. Soft results met with buying is absorption -- demand lurking under the surface. Watch the price action after the prints, not the headlines about beats and misses.

## To be fair

Calm can last a long time. Coiled markets can drift sideways for weeks, frustrating everyone waiting for the break. Compression resolves on its own schedule, not the calendar's -- earnings week is a candidate catalyst, not a guaranteed one.

And remember what low volatility actually is: a description of conditions, not a forecast. It tells you the market is quiet. It says nothing about what happens next. Some of the sharpest moves in market history started from exactly this kind of quiet.

## Here's your plan

1. **Don't predict the break -- define it.** A decisive move back above the 50-day average on expanding volume means the uptrend is reasserting itself. A break below the 200-day means the coil is releasing to the downside. Know your lines before the market tests them.
2. **Watch volatility, not just prices.** If 20-day realized volatility jumps from 10% toward 15% or higher, the quiet period is over. That's your signal to reassess position sizes, in either direction.
3. **Keep new commitments modest.** This is a market to react to, not to front-run. Half-size new positions until the trend gauges agree with each other again.

The spring is wound. This week may be what releases it -- or the market may just keep coiling. Either way, you'll be ready if you know what you're watching for.
