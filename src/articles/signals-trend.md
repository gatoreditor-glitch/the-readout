In October 2007, the S&P 500 closed below its 200-day moving average for the first time in years. It would not close back above it for over a year. Investors who treated that break as a regime change -- not a dip to buy -- sidestepped the worst of what followed.

That is the entire idea behind the trend regime gauge: price location relative to its own history tells you what kind of market you're in.

## What it measures

Two moving averages. The 50-day captures the intermediate trend -- roughly two and a half months of trading. The 200-day captures the long-term trend -- about ten months. Together they answer one question: is the market in an uptrend, a downtrend, or stuck between the two?

On this dashboard the rule is simple:

- **Bullish:** price above both the 50-day and the 200-day. Short-term and long-term crowds agree.
- **Bearish:** price below both. Nobody is defending this market.
- **Neutral:** price between them -- above one, below the other. The two timeframes are arguing, and arguing markets deserve smaller commitments.

## Why it works

Trends persist because big money moves slowly. Pension funds, endowments, and systematic strategies can't reposition overnight, so once capital starts flowing in one direction it tends to keep flowing. Moving averages are just a way to see that flow without the daily noise. This is the oldest form of systematic investing there is -- traders were following trends with hand-drawn charts a century before computers.

## To be fair

Moving averages are lagging by construction. They will never catch the exact top or bottom -- by the time price crosses the 200-day, a good chunk of the move has already happened. And in sideways, choppy markets they whipsaw: 2015 and 2016 produced repeated crosses in both directions that would have chopped a strict trend-follower to pieces. The gauge describes the regime accurately. It does not predict when the regime changes.

## How to use it

Treat trend regime as a filter, not a trigger. In a bullish regime, give your long ideas more room and more size; the wind is at your back. In a bearish regime, tighten risk, demand better entries, and respect that rallies tend to fail. In a neutral regime -- like a market below its 50-day but above its 200-day -- stay selective. The regime doesn't tell you what to buy. It tells you how aggressively to play whatever you find.
