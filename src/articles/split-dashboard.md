The dashboard just refreshed, and it reads like a market that can't make up its mind. Three gauges lean bullish, two sit neutral, and one glows bearish red. That split is the story -- the averages hide a real tension between calm conditions and fading momentum.

## The numbers

SPY sits at $766.48, below its 50-day average but still above the 200-day. Momentum reads +2.4% over the last three months -- positive, but barely moving. The trend gauge calls it neutral, and that's fair: the price is drifting, not breaking.

Meanwhile, the background conditions look fine. Realized volatility is just 8.7% annualized -- genuinely calm. The 10-year minus 2-year spread is +0.35 percentage points, a normal upward slope with no recession warning from the curve. Unemployment holds at 4.2%, flat over the last three months.

And then there's the one red gauge: inflation at 3.7% year over year. Bearish. It's the number that keeps the Fed hawkish and puts a ceiling on how excited anyone should get about the other five.

## What's really going on

Strip the labels and you get a simple picture: a market that isn't panicking and isn't charging, either. Volatility this low usually means complacency is the risk. But momentum this flat -- +2.4% over a quarter -- means there's no evidence of complacency running wild. The base-rate database backs up the caution: three straight down months and 10% drawdowns have historically been launchpads, but this market is neither. It's in between, and in-between markets resolve in the direction of the next piece of real news.

To be fair, there's a genuinely good case that this is just digestion. The labor market is steady at 4.2%, the curve is healthy, and vol at 8.7% is what healthy consolidation looks like. Markets that sit calmly below the 50-day without breaking the 200-day are often just catching their breath before the trend reasserts. The base-rate data on losing the 200-day (87% positive over six months, median +13.2%) argues that even a deeper break has tended to be a buying opportunity since 2010.

## What to watch

Two things decide whether this resolves up or down. First, the 50-day average -- a reclaim would flip the trend gauge from neutral back toward bullish and give the calm-volatility reading something constructive to work with. Second, the inflation print: CPI has crept from 3.3% six months ago to 3.7% now, and with U.S. crude holding above $100, the next reading has a real chance to keep climbing. That's the gauge that decides whether the other five get any help.

## Here's your plan

1. Don't mistake calm for safe -- 8.7% vol with flat momentum is a "wait for confirmation" market, not a signal to chase.
2. Watch SPY versus its 50-day line as your first trigger. A clean reclaim above it changes the dashboard's math.
3. Keep one eye on energy prices. At 3.7% CPI and rising, the red gauge is the one with veto power over everything else.
