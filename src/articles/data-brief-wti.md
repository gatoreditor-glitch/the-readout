The headlines are chasing crude above $100 a barrel. The chart tells the fuller story: this is a move that started in January, when WTI traded near $57, and it hasn't really stopped since.

Here's the year, built fresh from FRED's daily WTI series this morning:

![WTI crude oil price, 2026 year to date](/assets/charts/wti-ytd.png)

## The numbers

- **January 2: $57.21** -- where the year opened.
- **Year-to-date low: $56.01** -- nearly the same place, which means the run is essentially uninterrupted.
- **Latest FRED observation (Oct 14): $101.91** -- FRED's daily series runs a few days behind the tape.
- **Live tape this morning: WTI futures $98-$104** -- depending on the contract, right around the FRED print. The story is the trend, not a one-day blowoff.
- **Year-to-date high: $114.58** -- so today's move, while sharp, is still below the year's peak.
- **Net change: +78%** from the January open ($57.21) to the latest print ($101.91).

One chart, one fact: oil didn't spike today. It spiked over nine months, and today's tension premium is piled on top of a $44-a-barrel trend move.

## Why it matters now

The dashboard's lone bearish gauge is inflation at 3.7% year over year -- up from 3.3% six months ago. Oil above $100 is the most direct threat to that trajectory. Crude flows into gasoline, diesel freight, airfares, and plastics, and those flows show up in the CPI with a lag of a few months. The inflation brief yesterday noted the slow climb back to 3.7%; energy is the accelerant that could turn a climb into a sprint.

To be fair, the chart itself contains the bull case for oil optimists: the move is orderly, not vertical. A nine-month grind from $57 to $100-plus looks like tightening supply meeting resilient demand, not a panic bid. And the year's high of $114.58 shows this level has been tested before -- it's not uncharted territory, it's a return visit.

## Here's your plan

1. Separate the trend from the spike. The $57-to-$101 move is fundamentals; anything above that is tension premium. Size your worry accordingly.
2. Mark the next two CPI reports. Energy pass-through is where today's headline becomes tomorrow's Fed headache.
3. Note who's winning inside your own screen: in a $100-plus oil tape, upstream and service exposure has a tailwind, and energy-heavy margins have a headwind. The chart makes the case for checking which side your holdings are on.
