Google spent more than a decade building a chip business it would not share. On Tuesday, a day before Alphabet's third-quarter earnings report, it announced that Oracle will offer Google's tensor processing units, the custom AI chips Google has been developing since 2013. It is the first time customers will be able to get TPUs outside Google's own infrastructure.

Read that twice, because the reversal is the story. Google's long pitch was the full stack: buy its chips, its models, and its cloud together. Now Google is unbundling the chip from the cloud and handing distribution to a rival cloud provider. A company does not give up bundle control unless the prize for reach is bigger than the prize for control.

## The numbers behind the unbundling

The prize, by the analysts' estimates, is large and mostly still in the future. TD Cowen models $9.4 billion in TPU sales for the second half of 2026 and $62.5 billion for 2027; Wells Fargo expects about $9 billion in third-quarter sales and $99 billion next year. Google's finance chief said on the July earnings call that only a small portion of TPU revenue from existing agreements will be recognized this year, with the vast majority coming in 2027. Today's announcement is a down payment on a 2027 story, not a 2026 revenue event.

The cloud context explains the urgency. Google Cloud grew 82% in the second quarter to $25 billion in revenue, the fastest growth of the big three, but still trailing Amazon Web Services at $42 billion and Microsoft Azure at $29 billion. A chip business sold only inside its own cloud can only grow as fast as its own cloud does. Selling TPUs through Oracle breaks that ceiling.

Then there is the customer list, which is stranger than the partner list. Anthropic, described as Google's biggest TPU customer, agreed last October to use up to 1 million TPUs in a deal worth tens of billions of dollars -- while its Claude models compete directly with Google's Gemini. OpenAI is also planning to buy TPUs from Google. Add Broadcom's $63 billion financing vehicle, which buys TPUs and networking gear and leases them to Anthropic for five years, covering about half of Anthropic's 2027 needs by one analyst estimate. Google is now, in effect, a supplier to its own fiercest model rivals.

## Why a supplier to rivals is a systems signal, not just a deal

Here is the systems read: when a company starts selling its crown-jewel component to competitors, it is telling you where it thinks the durable advantage lives. Google's bet, on this evidence, is that the advantage is in the silicon and the stack optimization, not in keeping the silicon exclusive. SemiAnalysis estimates the latest Ironwood TPU costs about 44% less to own and operate than an Nvidia GB200 server. If those economics hold, exclusivity was leaving money on the table. Distribution becomes the moat, not secrecy.

## To be fair: the chip business is a worse business than the cloud business

The counterpoint is in the margins. TD Cowen estimates a 20% operating margin on external TPU sales, against about 35% for the cloud overall last quarter. Every dollar of chip revenue Google adds through a partner is a lower-margin dollar than a dollar of cloud revenue, and a customer who can buy your chip from Oracle has one less reason to buy your cloud. That trade only wins if the volume -- the $62.5 billion to $99 billion range of 2027 estimates -- actually arrives, and on the timeline the finance chief laid out, the proof is a year away.

## Here's your plan

Do not grade this announcement on today's headlines. Grade it on Alphabet's earnings report and the two numbers that follow it. First, how much TPU revenue is actually recognized, and does management repeat that the vast majority still lands in 2027? Second, does the Oracle channel produce genuinely new TPU customers, or the same names through a new door? New names mean distribution is working. The same names mean Google just rearranged its existing demand. That distinction is the whole trade.
