There's a moment in every market recovery when the debate ends. The S&P 500 has been living below its 200-day average for weeks -- sometimes months -- and then, one day, it closes back above it. The technicians call it the reclaim. Since 2010 it's happened eight times (counting only reclaims after at least 20 days below). A year later the market was higher 88% of the time, with a median gain of +14.9%.

## The numbers

- **Setup:** the S&P 500 closes back above its 200-day average after spending 20 or more trading days below it.
- **What followed:** a median gain of **+14.9%** over the next 12 months.
- **Hit rate:** positive **88%** of the time -- 7 out of 8.
- **Sample:** 8 episodes, 2010 through 2026.

## What it means

The reclaim is the market's all-clear siren. Twenty-plus days below the 200-day means the decline was real -- not a one-day shakeout but a genuine stretch of weakness. Climbing back above it means buyers have absorbed everything the sellers had and are now in charge. It's the mirror image of the first-break setup: where the break rewards courage, the reclaim rewards patience. By the time it fires, the bottom is usually in and a new advance is underway.

## The fine print

Eight episodes is a small sample, and the 20-day qualifier matters -- without it, you'd be counting every minor wiggle across the line, and the signal would dissolve into noise. Also note the trade-off versus buying the break itself: the reclaim is *safer* (confirmation in hand) but *later* (you've missed the first leg of the recovery). The +14.9% median twelve-month gain is excellent, but it starts from a higher base than the fear-driven entries. You're paying for certainty, and historically it's been worth the price.

## How to use it

This is the setup for investors who missed the bottom and need a disciplined re-entry. Rather than chasing every rally, wait for the reclaim: it has marked the resumption of the primary uptrend in 7 of 8 cases. Use it to upgrade your posture -- from defensive to constructive, from holding cash to putting it to work. And if the reclaim fails and price slips back below the line within days, respect it: failed reclaims are rare in this sample, but they're the market telling you the sellers aren't done.
