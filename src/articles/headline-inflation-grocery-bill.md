Inflation reports keep showing improvement. Grocery receipts keep telling a different story. The disconnect isn't a conspiracy — it's arithmetic.

## Inflation measures change, not levels

When economists say inflation "cooled" to 3%, they mean prices are rising 3% faster than a year ago — not that prices fell back to where they were. The big price jumps of recent years are baked into the level; slower inflation just means the pain is growing more slowly.

## Your basket isn't the average basket

The official index weights housing, transportation, healthcare, and hundreds of other categories. If your spending skews toward food and rent — as it does for most households — your personal inflation rate can run well above the headline number.

## What to watch instead

Economists increasingly look at the price level relative to pre-pandemic trends, not just the year-over-year rate. By that measure, the gap between headlines and lived experience makes perfect sense — and it explains why "cooling inflation" rarely feels like relief at the register.
