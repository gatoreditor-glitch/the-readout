When the Federal Reserve announces its interest rate decision, the headline number is usually the least interesting part. Markets have typically priced in the expected move weeks in advance. The real action is in the details around it.

## The statement

The post-meeting statement is parsed word by word. A single adjective change — from "elevated" to "moderating," for example — can shift expectations for the entire year. Analysts keep redlined versions of consecutive statements for exactly this reason.

## The dot plot and the press conference

The dot plot, released quarterly, shows where each policymaker expects rates to go. When the median dot moves, bond markets move. Then comes the press conference, where the chair's tone — hawkish or dovish — often matters more than anything on paper.

## Why it matters beyond Wall Street

Rate expectations feed directly into mortgage rates, auto loans, and credit card APRs. A Fed decision day is one of the few scheduled events that can move both a 401(k) and a monthly budget in the same afternoon.
