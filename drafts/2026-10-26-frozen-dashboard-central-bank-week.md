Nothing on the dashboard moved overnight. That is the story, because this is the week that moves dashboards.

A frozen dashboard is not a calm market. ## What the freeze is telling you

When every gauge holds still at once, the market is not undecided. It is priced for a specific outcome and unwilling to reprice until it hears otherwise. The current price says: growth steady enough, inflation hot enough, jobs solid enough. Nobody needs to change their mind today. Everybody may need to change it by Friday.

The gauge with the shortest fuse is the trend gauge. SPY at $759.97 lives in the hallway between the 50-day average overhead and the 200-day underneath. A hawkish surprise pushes it toward the floor. A dovish surprise pushes it toward the ceiling. Either way, the hallway is not where this market ends the week -- it is where it starts it.

The second fuse is the curve. A +0.31 point slope between the 10-year and the 2-year is a normal, upward-sloping curve, and it is the bond market's way of saying no recession warning today. Central-bank language moves that slope first and stock prices second. If the slope compresses sharply this week, treat it as the bond market disagreeing with the stock market, and the bond market has a better record in that argument.

## To be fair: the hot number is not the whole board

Inflation at 3.7% is the bearish gauge, and it is the reason the Fed cannot simply ride to the rescue. But it sits next to a labor market holding around 4.1-4.2% unemployment -- cooled, not cracking -- and volatility at 9.5%, which is genuinely quiet. Hot prices plus steady jobs is a constraint on rate cuts. Hot prices plus rising layoffs would be a threat to earnings. The dashboard is showing the first problem, not the second, and that distinction is worth more than any single headline this week.

Momentum at +2.8% over three months fits the same picture. It is positive without being convincing, the number you get when buyers and sellers both have a case and neither has a catalyst. This week hands them one.

## Here's your plan

Do not trade the meetings. Trade the gauges after them. Check three things in order when the week is done: first, is SPY back above the 50-day or through the 200-day -- one of those two lines, not the noise in between; second, did the curve slope widen or compress, and by how much; third, did volatility stay near 9.5% or jump, because a volatility jump after a frozen week tells you the repricing was real rather than a one-day flinch.

If none of the six gauges flips, the frozen dashboard was the right call and your patience was the trade. If two or more flip together, believe the move -- dashboards that sit still for days rarely thaw one needle at a time.
