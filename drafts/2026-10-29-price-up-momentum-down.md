## Why a rising price can carry falling momentum

This is rolling-window arithmetic, and it is worth understanding because it will fool you again. A 63-day return is not a measure of today. It is a measure of the last 63 days, and every day the window rolls forward: one old day drops out and one new day comes in. If the day that drops out was a strong one, the window's total can fall even when today's price rises. Price tells you where you are. Momentum tells you how you got here over a quarter. Right now those two answers disagree.

That disagreement is more useful than either gauge alone. A market rising on cooling three-month momentum is a market whose recent gains are doing less of the work than its older gains were. It does not mean the uptrend is broken -- $4.31 above the 50-day line says it is not -- but the trend's recent fuel is thinner than yesterday's dashboard suggested. Yesterday's flip looked like confirmation. Today's flip back says the confirmation was premature.

## To be fair: one gauge cooling is not a sell signal

The counterpoint matters here, because momentum gauges are noisy by construction. A 63-day window is sensitive to exactly which days roll in and out, and a single-day move from +5.5% to +4.1% can reflect window mechanics more than a genuine change in buyer behavior. The trend gauge, which uses price levels rather than a rolling return, actually improved over the same 24 hours. Reading today's flip as a reversal call would repeat yesterday's mistake in reverse: treating one day's gauge move as a verdict when it is a data point.

The honest read is narrower: the dashboard went from "everything but inflation agrees" to "everything but inflation agrees, except momentum." That is a downgrade in confidence, not a change in direction.
