Lockheed Martin is up 3% pre-open, and the headline answer to "why" is actually two answers stacked on top of each other. Per Investing.com's reporting this morning, the pop follows a Q3 earnings double beat AND a 10-year F-35 production framework agreement with the Pentagon. Get the attribution right -- it's the beat plus the deal, not one or the other.

([Source: Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-is-lockheed-martin-stock-surging-today-93CH-4965500))

The earnings half is straightforward: adjusted EPS of $7.97 against $7.52 consensus, revenue of $20.7 billion against $20.48 billion expected, up 11% year over year, net earnings up 14% to $1.845 billion, and full-year 2026 EPS guidance raised. That's a clean beat in a sector where clean beats are the norm -- defense contractors rarely surprise on the downside when the customer is the U.S. government.

## The number that matters: $292 billion

The deal half is where the systems read lives. Lockheed's order backlog hit a record $292 billion, up 27% sequentially -- and that backlog is the business. In defense, revenue doesn't get forecast, it gets contracted. A backlog that size, growing that fast, is forward revenue with a signature on it.

Inside that number: a $52.5 billion PAC-3 MSE interceptor award, and the new F-35 framework covering at least 850 aircraft across production Lots 21-30 at a cadence of 156 jets per year. Lockheed also committed to self-fund $7 billion in F-35 initiatives, and raised free-cash-flow guidance to $7.8-$8.0 billion.

Here's the systems lens: defense is the one sector where a stock move should be read against backlog, not multiples. Multiples argue about sentiment. Backlog measures signed demand. A 27% sequential jump in backlog is the strongest fundamental signal this sector produces.

## To be fair: a framework is not a contract

A 10-year framework agreement sets the shape of a deal -- 850+ aircraft, 156 per year -- but frameworks get repriced, renegotiated, and occasionally re-litigated over a decade. The $7 billion in self-funded F-35 initiatives is Lockheed's money at risk before the government's money arrives. And the 3% move prices in both the certain (this quarter's beat) and the contingent (ten years of aircraft deliveries). Those deserve different discounts.

## Here's your plan

Stop watching the stock and start watching the backlog conversion. The two numbers that decide whether this morning's 3% was cheap or expensive: the F-35 delivery cadence (156 a year is roughly 13 a month -- watch for slip) and whether that $292 billion backlog converts into the promised $7.8-$8.0 billion of free cash flow. Backlog is a promise. Cash flow is the receipt. Collect the receipt.
