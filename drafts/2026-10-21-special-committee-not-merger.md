Markets have a long habit of pricing press releases at the certainty of done deals and then spending months repricing back down to the actual probability. ## The number to keep in your head: zero terms

As of this morning, there are no terms. No exchange ratio, no premium, no structure, no timeline. Nobody -- not Musk, not the committees, not JPMorgan's analysts who are flagging what they call the single biggest obstacle to a deal -- can tell you what a share of Tesla gets you in this transaction or what a SpaceX private shareholder receives.

That is not a criticism of the idea. There is genuine industrial logic here: Starlink is a cash-generating subscription business sitting inside a rocket company, and Tesla is a manufacturing and robotics machine that keeps getting valued as a car company. Put the two together and you can tell a story about the first vertically integrated space-industrial conglomerate. It is a good story.

But systems traders don't trade stories. They trade expected value, and expected value requires a probability and a price. Right now we have neither. What we have is a sentiment event -- five headlines deep before lunch -- and sentiment events are for watching, not chasing.

## To be fair

Sometimes the press release *is* the trade. Special committees exploring a deal, announced by the principal himself, do not form for nothing. Musk doesn't volunteer his companies into months of legal process as a thought experiment. The base case has to be that a real transaction is being seriously evaluated, which means today's buyers are probably not wrong about the direction -- only about the certainty.

And there is precedent for the industrial logic winning. The market has a habit of initially discounting Musk combinations (the SolarCity deal was widely mocked at announcement) and then being embarrassed by the outcome. Betting against the combination on vibes has not been the high-percentage play historically.

One caveat worth stating plainly: this morning's 8% pop is not pure merger sentiment. The stock also posted a Q3 earnings beat, so the move reflects earnings momentum as well as merger speculation -- worth keeping straight before extrapolating the market's enthusiasm for the deal itself.

Don't chase the premarket pop. An 8% move with zero published terms is pure probability repricing -- you're paying the "inevitable" price for a deal Musk himself calls uncertain.

If this becomes a real deal with real terms, it will be a multi-month story with multiple entry points. You are not going to miss it by skipping day one.
