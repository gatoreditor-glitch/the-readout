## The one gauge that refuses to join

Inflation is the holdout, and it is not close. CPI year over year sits at 3.7%, and the gauge reads bearish because a print that hot keeps the Fed hawkish. That is the tension inside today's dashboard in one sentence: five gauges say the market's trend, momentum, calm, curve, and labor backdrop all support risk, and the inflation gauge says the central bank still has a reason to say no.

That split is worth more attention than the headline count of five-to-one. A market can climb with hot inflation for a while -- it is doing so right now, with momentum at +5.5% -- but hot inflation caps how far the Fed can help if something breaks. The bullish gauges describe the market as it is. The bearish gauge describes the policy room if it stops being that way.

## To be fair: one day above a line is the weakest bullish signal there is

The counterpoint is the $1.66 itself. Moving averages are magnets as much as they are floors: prices cross them, get pulled back, and cross them again, and each crossing generates a fresh round of "trend restored" commentary that ages badly within a week. A single close above the 50-day, on the eve of a Fed decision, is exactly the kind of flip that can reverse before the ink dries. The honest read is that the dashboard improved, not that it resolved. Improvement and resolution are different things, and only the second one deserves new money.
